Reference card

Print this card in a light, matte filament. Light blue or aqua works best because it stands out from both brass keys and the white glare on silver keys. Matte white is the next best choice. Avoid silk or glossy filament, grey, and yellow or gold.

The key lies flat in the recess for the side photos. The notches along the bottom edge measure the key's thickness: push the blade edge-on into the notches, and the narrowest one it fits is its thickness. The number beside each notch is the size, so 16 means 1.6 mm.

The round keyhole window at the left of the recess is for the keyhole photo: hold the card flat against the lock with the keyhole showing through it. For the side photos, lay the key with its bow over the window and its tip pointing the way of the raised arrow.

After printing: measure the card with calipers and type in the real width and height, plus the floor thickness and recess depth (together they make the total thickness).

The numbers are raised 0.4 mm, so print with a fine layer height (0.12–0.16 mm) to keep them crisp.

Checking the notches: printers can make holes slightly too small or too big. If something you know the thickness of reads wrong, change the notch width adjustment (for example +0.05 if notches come out tight) and reprint.

Using a real bank card instead? Set the recess depth to 0 and the floor thickness to the card's thickness (usually 0.76 mm).

Phone screen (for the decoder)

The on-screen decoder draws the key life-size, so the app needs to know how wide your screen really is. Pick your phone, then press Check size and lay your printed card on the outline. If it isn't an exact fit, nudge the fine-tune until it is.

Taking good photos

  • Shoot straight down from about 30–40 cm. Use 2× zoom if your phone has it.
  • Use soft, even light. Hard light gives shadows, and shadows get read as part of the key.
  • Put the card on a dark surface.
  • Side A: key face up in the recess. Side B: flip it over and photograph again.

🔑 Key decoder

Decode a key on your phone screen, no photos needed. Lay the key flat on the screen and match the outline to it: the cuts first, then the grooves on each side. The key is then built perfectly from its code, with exact cut positions, depths and clean V-cuts. Set your phone model on the ⚙︎ page first.

Custom: open the decoder on screen and add the cuts one at a time with + Cut, sliding each one up or down onto its cut and setting its depth. Save as system there turns it into a reusable key system for that brand.

Groove depth uses the Auto grooves depth setting (% of thickness) on the Keyway step. The bow is the one chosen on the Build step.

Keyway profile

The side photos give the key's outline. The keyway profile is the cross-section of the blade, including its grooves. Choose how to get it:

Edge photo

The card's front edge is the ruler. With the camera level with the card, you look straight at the end of the key, and the bow behind it looks smaller, so the blade's outline stands out.

  1. Lay the key flat on the card, side A up, with the cut edge on your right as you face the card's edge. Push it forward until the tip is level with the card's front edge.
  2. Put something plain and dark about 10 cm behind, such as a cloth or a sheet of card. Don't hold the key down with a finger; use a dab of Prestik under the bow if it moves.
  3. Hold the phone level with the card, looking straight at the front edge. Get close, or use 2× zoom, so the card fills the width of the photo, and tap the key to focus.
  4. Light from the front makes the end of the key bright and the grooves dark.
  5. Drag the four handles onto the corners of the card's front edge strip (top-left, top-right, bottom-right, bottom-left), then tap the end of the key to place the K marker.
  1. Hold the card flat against the front of the lock with the keyhole showing in the middle of the card's round window.
  2. Photograph straight on, with the whole card in the frame.
  3. Place the four corner handles. The app zooms straight to the window. (On a card without a window, tap the keyhole to place the K marker.)

Drag the handles onto the card's outer corners. Tap the key's end (or the keyhole) to place the K marker.

Trace the profile

The app starts at the K marker and spreads out to pixels of a similar colour. If it picked the wrong thing, choose Seed and tap on the key's end (or inside the keyhole). Pinch to zoom in (double-tap to fit), then build or trim the profile with squares and circles: ■ + / ● + add, ■ − / ● − remove. Drag from corner to corner for a square, or from the centre outwards for a circle.

Auto grooves (side photos)

Grooves are found in your side A and B photos. Their depth can't be seen from above, so each groove is cut to a set share of the key's thickness. Halfway is the default, which makes the copy slightly smaller than the original in those spots so it slides in easily. Grooves on opposite faces that line up are kept from cutting through, with at least the minimum wall left between them.

Manual grooves

Describe the grooves on each face. Position is measured from the bottom (uncut) edge of the blade to the groove's centre. Auto-detect fills in where the grooves are and how wide they are from your side photos. Depth can't be seen from above, so each groove is given the assumed depth. Adjust the depths using calipers or by comparing with the lock.

Bow

The bow is always a clean designed shape. Camera is a camera silhouette with a lens hole, with the blade coming out of its bottom edge. Circle and octagon have a 5 mm keyring hole near the top and a short shoulder block. Either way, the shoulder sits exactly where your key's shoulder was, so the key still stops at the right depth. Size is the camera width, or the circle or octagon diameter. The small rounding where the blade meets the bow makes the join stronger. Set it to 0 if the key won't go all the way in.

Side A vs side B

Green = side A only, magenta = side B only, white = both. The blades are lined up on their own, so a mismatched bow doesn't matter (it's replaced). Thin coloured edges are normal. Thick coloured areas on the blade mean one photo traced it differently.

Blade check

Red line = shoulder (the keyway profile applies from here to the tip). Green lines = blade bottom and top. The cut edge should be at the top. With Cut edge cleanup on, the faint red shows what was trimmed from the photo trace and the faint green shows what was filled in.
